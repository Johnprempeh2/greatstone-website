# Everest + personalised AI intelligence · strategy

*Prepared for John. Decision-ready. Grounded in the two existing plans (docs/AI-INTEL-HUB-PLAN.md, docs/ANALYTICS-CRM-PLAN.md) and the live stack (React + Puck on Netlify, Supabase + four edge functions, Stripe test, the dormant analytics/CRM rails).*

---

## The concept

The reference dashboard answered one question for one reader: should *Greatstone* care about this AI development. The proposal is to keep the engine and flip the reader. Four ideas, and they form one product ladder rather than four bets.

1. **The internal to external reframe.** The same human-published briefing, run through a customer-context block instead of an internal one. Where the internal take asked "should we build, watch, or ignore this", the client take asks "what this means for your 8-pillar profile and your sector". It is a prompt and context swap on the existing hybrid engine, not a new engine.

2. **The personalised feed.** A signed-in client does not see the chronological briefing list. They see the same published items re-ranked against a per-company interest vector: their Deep Dive pillar scores, their sector, their declared stack, and what they have read before. The feed does not generate content per user. It re-orders and re-frames content that a human already approved.

3. **The on-demand company-effect report.** One click on a briefing ("what does this mean for us?") or a standing "generate my AI landscape report" produces a structured, fixed-shape artefact: the news item, the published Greatstone take, the areas of their profile it may touch, and the questions worth exploring. It reads like a Greatstone deliverable, not a chatbot dump, and it ends on a soft pointer to the paid work.

4. **Everest.** The conversational shell that unifies feed, report, account, and catalogue into one assistant that guides, helps, and (carefully) upsells. The feed and the report are not separate features that Everest replaces. They are Everest's first two organs, shipped and proven before the mouth is attached.

The single honest framing to hold throughout: **this is a sales-intelligence and re-engagement play wearing a thought-leadership coat.** The feed is table stakes that gives clients a reason to return. The profile-conditioned report is the actual moat and the actual conversion event. Everest is a vision, not a near-term build. Judge the whole thing on pipeline influenced and upsell conversations opened, not on pageviews.

## How it works

### The structural dependency that comes first

Everything here is gated on data that does not exist yet. The Deep Dive form (`BookDeepDive.jsx`) is a prototype that persists **nothing**, it shows a confirmation and never transmits. So the 8-pillar profile that powers the reframe, the feed ranking, and the report has no home in the schema today (migrations stop at quotes/invoicing; there is no deep-dive, pillar, assessment, or maturity table). The good news is that storing it needs **no new auth and no new RLS pattern**: `customer_users.auth_subject` is the existing Supabase-auth join, and `getCustomerProfile()` already resolves a signed-in buyer to their `customers` row. The profile bolts onto `customers.metadata` (JSONB) for a v1 ship today, or a thin `deep_dive_profile` table keyed on `customer_id` if you want versioning and history (recommended, because a stale snapshot is a liability, see Risks). Seeding is an admin write in the studio for v1, mirroring the `request-quote` service-role insert pattern. No self-serve scoring engine is needed to start.

### Architecture sketch

The expensive infrastructure already exists and is the *same* plumbing these features need: server-side edge functions holding secrets, the customer auth/RLS join, the draft-to-publish content gate, the propose-to-human-approve `ai_actions` pattern, and the dormant analytics/attribution layer. The genuinely new work is narrow.

- **Feed ranking** is pure arithmetic, no LLM. The hub plan already fixes a small topic taxonomy (foundation models, agents, AI security, coding, enterprise adoption, regulation, funding, infra, multimodal) and tags every briefing. That taxonomy *is* the personalisation key. Score each briefing = topic match to profile interests + pillar-derived weights + sector match + stack/vendor match + recency. The pillar-to-topic map (low Data pillar boosts data/governance items; security-weak boosts ai-security) is a static lookup authored once. This runs client-side over the already-fetched published dataset (it is tiny) or in a `personalised-feed` edge function.

- **The customer "for your business" framing** is **pre-generated by the daily job per sector cohort**, not per user per view. Cluster profiles into the eight existing sector buckets so the job drafts (say) six to eight takes per item, not N. The read-time personalisation is ranking plus a light profile overlay, which is cheap ordering logic with zero per-view LLM cost.

- **The on-demand report** is one new LLM edge function using **RAG-by-context-stuffing, no vector store**. The entire published intel corpus is roughly 8k to 15k tokens, it fits in one prompt. Building pgvector here solves a problem you do not have (the repo has only tsvector for product search). The report stuffs the corpus + the user's profile + the specific item into one call, returns a fixed-shape artefact, and logs it to a `reports` table (mirroring the request-quote capture pattern). Because it is a discrete, logged artefact rather than always-on output, it is the *safer* place for deep per-company claims than the live feed.

- **Everest** is the assembly layer: a chat-orchestration edge function with tool-use that calls the same profile/intel/catalogue reads as tools. This is the largest and most speculative build.

### Model split

- **Cheap (Haiku-class):** routine chat, feed framing, ranking assists.
- **Mid (Sonnet-class):** the daily customer takes and standard on-demand reports.
- **Frontier (Opus-class):** reserved for Everest's harder multi-turn reasoning and a premium "deep" report variant only.

### MVP vs full vision

| | MVP (pilot) | Full vision |
|---|---|---|
| **Profile** | Admin-seeded onto `customers.metadata` | Versioned `deep_dive_profile` table, freshness-gated |
| **Feed** | Re-rank published items, "why you're seeing this" chip | "Tune your feed" controls writing back to profile |
| **Reports** | One templated report function, sign-in gated, rate-limited | Premium deep variant, saved report history |
| **Everest** | Not built | Streaming assistant, tool-use, conversation storage, upsell triggers |
| **Sales signal** | Manual; report generation logged | Pipedrive interest-profile roll-up (rides CRM go-live) |

## Ways it benefits Greatstone

Each mechanism with its causal chain, marked proven or speculative.

**1. Re-engagement of captured leads, PROVEN, near-term.**
Mechanism: a daily-fresh, profile-ranked feed gives an already-captured lead a standing reason to return weekly between sales touches, turning one-shot visitors into a recurring audience the sales motion can work. This is the defensible audience mechanism. Note the inverse: as raw *cold* lead-gen this is weak and should not be claimed, a small consultancy will not out-publish the AI-news incumbents, and a cold reader of one briefing is a thin lead.

**2. The upsell hook, PROVEN as a mechanism, but it must be deliberately blunted (see the critique below).**
Mechanism: the report converts a news event into a scoped diagnosis against a real profile and lands on a Greatstone next step. This is the cheapest qualified-lead generator Greatstone could build and it is on-brand, diagnosis-then-prescription is literally the Deep Dive method. **The sharp warning:** an LLM generating "here's what Greatstone would do" against a client's real pillars *automates the paid deliverable for free* and trains clients to expect the diagnosis as a feature. The report must therefore stop short of the diagnosis, it raises good questions and shows Greatstone is paying attention; it must not deliver recommendations. Reframed that way the benefit holds and the Deep Dive's price is protected. Left unreframed, this "benefit" is the flagship risk wearing a different hat.

**3. Lead qualification / intent signal, PROVEN technique, MODEST value here.**
Mechanism: every interaction fires a taxonomy-keyed event that rolls cleanly into a weighted interest profile; volume and recency are themselves a lead score, and the report-generation event is a strong intent signal. Honest caveat: intent scoring is a high-volume PLG technique, and Greatstone's funnel is the opposite shape, a handful of named, high-touch accounts where a salesperson on a live deal already knows what the account cares about. Treat this as a near-free annotation on a CRM record, not a qualification engine. Switch it on after the CRM go-live; never build *toward* it.

**4. Sales enablement, PROVEN mechanism, gated on credentials and volume.**
Mechanism: the report/engagement profile rolls into the Pipedrive Person as a field ("interests: AI security, model comparison; most-viewed vendor: Anthropic; last read: <headline>"). The rep opens the record and knows how to open the conversation. The rails are specced (ANALYTICS-CRM Layer 3). Gated on the Pipedrive token and a meaningful base of signed-in accounts.

**5. The proprietary data asset and the Everest moat, SPECULATIVE, treat as upside.**
The 8-pillar join is genuinely something no AI-news competitor holds. But it is a moat over an audience of clients who have done a paid Deep Dive *and* signed in *and* return, single to low-double digits near term, which is a feature for a few accounts, not a moat. Frame it as conditional upside, not as the business case. The defensible thing is Greatstone's ability to *act* (deliver the roadmap), not its ability to write a plausible take, which any well-prompted LLM does for free.

## What it costs

The architecture verdict is unambiguous and verified against the repo: **approve the design** (context-stuffing over a vector store is correct and cheaper). The cost *numbers* in the optimistic model, however, need three corrections before any sign-off.

**Correction 1, the cache is cold, so reports cost ~3x the optimistic figure.** The default prompt-cache TTL is 5 minutes. The corpus is written once a day and read by reports that arrive sparsely, so at 10 to 100 users you almost never get two reports inside one 5-minute window. The "billed at ~0.1x after the first call that day" claim is fiction, there is no warm cache 23 hours later. Effective input per report is ~16k tokens (cold corpus 12k + system 1.5k + profile 1.5k + item 1k), not the ~5-6k claimed. Honest uncached cost on a Sonnet-class model is ~$0.08/report, not ~$0.02. To actually buy the 0.1x read price you must run a scheduled keep-warm under the 5-minute TTL, which is roughly $90/month of pure keep-warm the optimistic model never lists. The corpus is not "effectively free".

**Correction 2, frontier model prices carry 2-3x risk.** The quoted "$5/$25 Opus" rates are recollection, not the published catalogue. Price every frontier line with a 2-3x sensitivity band, plus output inflation from always-on thinking. This is the argument for keeping frontier *only* for Everest and the premium report, never for routine paths.

**Correction 3, at scale, the dominant cost is human, not tokens.** The hybrid publish gate is ~10 to 15 minutes of senior editorial time per day regardless of user count. At low scale this fixed floor dwarfs the LLM bill. The product is **fixed-cost-heavy at low scale, cheaper per user at scale**, the opposite of the "cheap to run" headline.

### Corrected model (variable infra + LLM, with the human floor noted separately)

| Users | LLM + infra (variable) | + fixed editorial/human floor | Honest all-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | ~$10-25/mo | ~$400-1,000/mo | **Dominated by the human floor** |
| 100 | ~$40-90/mo | ~$400-1,000/mo | Still human-dominated |
| 1,000 | ~$300-700/mo | scales (support burden grows) | Tokens approach the floor |
| 10,000 | ~$2,500-6,500/mo | scales | Tokens now dominate |

Assumptions held: ~2 reports + ~3 Everest conversations per active user per month; feed pre-generated (NOT per-user, a bespoke take per user per item roughly 10-20x's the 10k case); reports on mid-tier with a frontier "deep" variant; Everest mid-tier with ~20% frontier escalation. **Build cost** adds the profile table + capture, the report edge function, and (for the full vision) the chat orchestration layer.

**The number that matters is not the steady-state bill. It is the unbounded blast radius until the controls exist.** Even the corrected higher figures are covered by a single Deep Dive upsell conversion, so the steady-state economics are fine. What is not fine is an unguarded endpoint.

### Mandatory cost controls (build gate, not backlog)

The function pattern proposed for reuse (`request-quote`) is currently deployed `--no-verify-jwt`, CORS `*`, with **zero rate-limiting anywhere** in the functions. An unauthenticated, publicly POST-able LLM endpoint is unbounded cost, denial-of-wallet is trivially exploitable. These five are pre-conditions for *any* cost number to be meaningful:

1. **Verified JWT** on the report/Everest functions (gate behind sign-in, already the plan).
2. **Locked CORS** to the production origin.
3. **Per-customer DB-enforced daily caps** on reports and conversations.
4. **A global spend circuit-breaker** that trips the function off above a daily ceiling.
5. **Bot protection** on the authenticated endpoints.

Plus: pre-generate the one shared sector narrative per cohort, cache the corpus prefix (with the keep-warm cost owned, not wished away), and tier the model.

## Risks + mitigations

The core structural problem is one sentence: **the existing hub plan is safe precisely because the daily job only ever writes a draft and a human publishes, and all three new features break that gate.** A personalised feed, an on-demand report, and Everest are by definition generated per user, in real time, at a volume no human can pre-review. You are taking the one piece the plan deliberately kept human-gated and making it autonomous and customer-specific. Every risk below flows from that inversion. The fix is to replace the human gate with *structural* controls (retrieval-grounding, templating, disclaimers, isolation), not "a better prompt".

And the stakes are higher here than for a generic chatbot, because **Greatstone sells trust and judgement**, the diagnostic and roadmap *are* the product. A consumer chatbot that hallucinates is an annoyance. A consultancy's branded assistant that tells a named client "this AI development threatens your Data pillar, you should act", wrongly, damages the exact asset being sold, and arguably constitutes professional advice.

| # | Risk | Sev / Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hallucination in a personalised report (the flagship).** Names the client, sounds authoritative, ends in an action, and is designed to upsell, the maximum-damage configuration. | High / High | Retrieval-grounded only: the model may state facts *only* from (a) the human-published intel dataset and (b) the user's own stored profile. Never parametric knowledge, never live web. Extend the plan's own principle ("never invents a headline or date") to "never invents a fact about the client". **Template the report** into fixed sections; frame the personalised part as hypotheses and questions ("areas of your profile this *may* touch"), never conclusions. This single move neutralises most of the hallucination *and* liability risk at once. |
| 2 | **Liability / unlicensed advice.** Branded, specific, actionable output reads as professional advice. | High / Med | Hard disclaimers; frame every output as "starting points for a conversation", never "advice" or "recommendations". The templating in #1 does most of the work. |
| 3 | **Denial-of-wallet.** Unauthenticated LLM endpoint = unbounded spend. | High / High | The five build-gate controls above. **Dealbreaker:** no LLM endpoint ships before verified JWT + per-user caps + global circuit-breaker are live. |
| 4 | **Cannibalisation of the paid Deep Dive.** A competent free "what this means + what to do" trains clients to expect the diagnosis as a feature. | High / Med | The report stops short of the diagnosis. It raises questions and shows attention; it does not deliver the prescription. The diagnosis stays paid. (Same control as #1, different motivation.) |
| 5 | **Stale-profile error.** A report conditioned on a 6-month-old pillar score is confidently wrong about the client's current state. | Med / High | Freshness-gate: personalise only if the Deep Dive is recent; otherwise fall back to sector-level framing. This is the argument for the versioned profile table. |
| 6 | **Unsolicited-alarm tone.** "This news threatens your Data pillar" pushed at a client reads alarmist and salesy, the opposite of calm authority. | Med / High | Make profile-conditioned output **pull** (client requests it) not **push** (no auto-alarms against their pillars). The feed personalises ordering; the deep claim only fires on a click. |
| 7 | **New data processor / privacy.** Deep Dive data hitting a third-party LLM is a new processor not on the named list (the analytics plan names GA/Pipedrive/Stripe). | High / High | Update the privacy policy named-processor list with a no-training contractual term and EU/UK residency or SCCs **before** any profile data reaches a model. Dealbreaker for go-live. |
| 8 | **Prompt injection / data scope.** Everest with tool-use and broad scope is an attack surface. | High / Med | Strict per-user RLS scope (only that user's row, service-role boundary), an output allow-list, treat all user input as hostile. This is why Everest ships *last*, after the controls are proven on the narrow report surface. |
| 9 | **Credibility decay of the feed.** Wrong, stale, or generic intel actively damages the trust the core business runs on. | Med / Med | The existing human-publish gate on the briefings themselves; the "updated <date>" honesty banner; explainable scores. |

**Dealbreakers (must be true before the matching surface goes live, not reasons to kill the idea):** the five cost controls (#3) before any LLM endpoint; the processor/privacy update (#7) before profile data touches a model; the report templated and grounded (#1) before it is customer-facing; Everest's scope and injection controls (#8) before a free-form agent goes live.

## The strongest case against (steelman)

Build none of it now, beyond finishing Phase A of the hub. The argument:

The whole thing is gated on an asset Greatstone does not have, a population of clients who have done a paid Deep Dive, signed in, and return weekly. Near term that is single digits. You would be building a personalisation engine, a report generator, and eventually a conversational agent for an audience of a handful of accounts a salesperson already knows by name and could brief in a ten-minute call. The "moat" is a feature for those few; the "intent data asset" is noise below a hundred accounts; the "always-on upsell" risks giving away a thin version of the paid diagnosis and training clients to expect it free.

Meanwhile the surface that creates value, the personalised report, is the single highest-risk thing Greatstone could ship: specific, authoritative, actionable, branded, and tuned to sell, which is exactly the configuration where one confident error does maximum reputational and legal damage to a business whose entire product is judgement. And it does this by removing the one control (human publish) that made the original hub safe. The cost is fixed-heavy and human-dominated at the scale Greatstone actually operates at, and the blast radius is unbounded until five controls that do not exist today are built. Everest specifically is a large build competing for the same attention as client delivery, and if it ever needs to be a real conversational agent, that layer should be bought, not built from scratch.

Conclusion of the steelman: finish the hub, switch on the analytics/CRM rails, and get the simple intent annotation into Pipedrive. Defer everything profile-conditioned until the client base justifies it.

This case is strong and is largely *right about sequencing*. Where it overreaches: it treats the report's risk as a reason not to build, when the same templating-plus-grounding control neutralises the risk *and* protects the Deep Dive price simultaneously. A narrow, templated, pull-only report over published data is genuinely low blast radius and is the cheapest warm-upsell generator Greatstone could own. The steelman wins on Everest and on timing; it loses on the report, provided the report is built as a constrained artefact rather than a free chatbot.

## Recommendation

**Phased, pilot-first. Go on the report; defer Everest.**

The honest call: this is a strong re-engagement and upsell engine and a weak media product, so build the upsell engine and skip the media ambitions. The four ideas do not ship together, they ship in the brief's own stated order, because the feed and report are precursors to Everest, and the narrow surface must prove the controls before the broad one exists.

**Pilot first (the only thing to build next):**
1. **The profile rail.** Make the Deep Dive persist, a versioned `deep_dive_profile` table, admin-seeded in the studio. Nothing downstream exists without this, and today it persists nothing.
2. **The templated, grounded, pull-only report.** One edge function, RAG-by-context-stuffing, fixed sections, hypotheses-not-conclusions, sign-in gated, with all five cost controls as a build gate and the privacy-processor update done first. Ship it to a handful of real Deep Dive clients as a manual, monitored pilot. Measure one thing: does a report open a real upsell conversation?

**Then, if the pilot earns it:**
3. **The personalised feed** as the hub's signed-in Overview, re-rank plus the "why you're seeing this" chip. Cheap, deterministic, no per-user LLM.
4. **The sector-cohort customer takes** drafted by the daily job (six to eight per item), which is the scalable form of the "for your business" reframe.

**Hold:**
5. **Everest** as a near-term build. Keep it as the stated vision and let the feed + report *be* its proven organs. If it ever becomes a conversational, task-doing agent, evaluate buying that layer rather than building a chat platform from scratch.

### How it sequences with the existing rails

- **AI-intel hub:** finish **Phase A** (the hub + seed dataset) and **Phase B** (the hybrid engine + Supabase draft/published) regardless, they are prerequisites and stand alone. The client reframe is a Phase B+ extension (the sector-cohort takes), not a parallel track.
- **Analytics/CRM:** the report and feed do **not** wait on the GTM id or Pipedrive token. The *sales-signal* benefits do. Switch the analytics rails on (Phase 1+2 are already shipped dormant, just set `VITE_GTM_ID`), then wire the Pipedrive roll-up (Phase 3) when the token lands, and let the report-generation event flow into it as a strong intent annotation. Order: profile rail → pilot report → analytics go-live → feed → Pipedrive intent roll-up → (much later) Everest.
